My Plan

A Vision for
Homestead's Future

Platform

Where Julio Stands

Homestead has a council-manager government. The mayor is one member of the City Council. The Council sets policy and adopts the budget, while the city manager directs employees and daily operations. Julio will bring proposals to the Council, work with the manager on implementation, and report back to residents on the results.

01

Faster Permits, Better City Services

A Business-Friendly Homestead

Less Red Tape. A City That Gets Things Done.

Homestead should be a city where residents, entrepreneurs, and businesses are welcomed, encouraged, and supported, not frustrated by unnecessary delays and complicated government processes.

Julio believes City Hall should be focused on solutions, not obstacles. Whether someone is opening a small business, improving their home, or investing in our community, they deserve a permitting and application process that is clear, efficient, predictable, and responsive.

Already at Work: Business Development Task Force

Julio is the Chair of the Homestead Business Development Task Force, a volunteer group of local builders, contractors, real estate professionals, business owners, and economic development leaders. Its charge is to recommend practical ways to make it easier to build, invest, and open a business in Homestead.

When the permitting process is too slow, we need to fix it. When departments lack the resources or technology to process applications efficiently, we need to address it. And when outdated or unnecessarily complicated city regulations are creating obstacles, Julio will work with the City Council to review and change them where appropriate, while maintaining essential safety standards.

First Steps

  • Use the task force’s recommendations to push for quick wins that the City can put in place right away.
  • Identify and eliminate unnecessary steps, duplicative reviews, and administrative delays in permitting and application processes.
  • Work with residents, business owners, contractors, and city staff to identify the biggest obstacles.
  • Evaluate staffing, technology, and departmental procedures to improve processing times.

Work With Council

  • Bring task force recommendations that need Council action, such as code, zoning, or fee changes, to the Council for a public vote.
  • Establish clear expectations and measurable goals for faster permit reviews and application processing.
  • Review outdated city codes and ordinances that unnecessarily delay responsible development, business openings, and property improvements.
  • Support a customer-service culture at City Hall where applicants receive clear guidance, timely communication, and practical solutions.

Measure Progress

  • Track permitting and application processing times to ensure meaningful improvements.
  • Hold city leadership accountable for improving efficiency and delivering measurable results.
  • Make adjustments when processes, resources, or city regulations continue to create unnecessary delays.

The goal is simple: Make Homestead a city where it's easier to open a business, improve a property, invest in our community, and get things done.

02

Public Safety

Safety

Every family in Homestead deserves to feel safe at home, at work, and in our parks. That starts with a well-staffed, well-trained police department and real trust between officers and the neighborhoods they serve.

Julio will support evidence-based safety priorities and ask for clear reporting on results.

First Steps

  • Review current crime and police data with police leadership and the city manager to pinpoint where Homestead needs improvement, and hear directly from residents in the neighborhoods most affected.
  • Push for competitive recruiting and retention so Homestead can hire and keep the officers it needs.

Work With Council

  • Champion budget priorities that keep officers trained, equipped, and focused on prevention, including crisis-response partnerships for mental health calls.
  • Build partnerships for after-school programs and mentorship so young people have positive options.

Measure Progress

  • Publish regular, easy-to-read safety reports so residents can see what’s working.
03

Traffic Relief

Traffic & Transportation

Homestead drivers are stuck: busway signal delays, a growing city, and state roads the City doesn’t control. Julio will be a loud, persistent voice for Homestead with Miami-Dade County and FDOT, and will make sure the City delivers on what it controls.

First Steps

  • Press Miami-Dade County to fix busway signal timing on Homestead’s cross streets, with clear deadlines and public updates. Make the busway work for drivers and riders alike.
  • Push to fund and build the projects in the City’s adopted Transportation Master Plan, starting with the 162nd Avenue bridge and East 6th Avenue improvements.

Work With Council

  • Make sure new development pays its fair share for the road improvements it requires.
  • Prioritize school-zone safety, crosswalks, and traffic calming on neighborhood streets.

Measure Progress

  • Publish a simple project tracker so residents can see what’s funded, what’s under construction, and what’s done.
04

New Employers & a Thriving Downtown

Economic Development

Homestead has real momentum. A $280 million privately funded sports campus is rising at Homestead Regional Park, with about 600 permanent jobs expected. Homestead Air Reserve Base is the candidate site for 24 new F-35A jets. Former base land is now designated Florida spaceport territory, and aviation employers like Global Engine Stands are moving in. Julio wants to turn that momentum into good jobs for Homestead residents and customers for Homestead businesses, starting downtown.

Bring New Employers

  • Lead an aerospace and aviation recruitment push built around Homestead Air Reserve Base and our spaceport territory, working with the base, Space Florida, Miami-Dade County, and the Economic Development Council of South Miami-Dade.
  • Put Homestead residents first in line for new jobs at the Sports Performance Hub, the reopened Amazon fulfillment center, and arriving employers.
  • Partner with Miami Dade College, CareerSource South Florida, and local schools to train residents for the jobs coming to Homestead.

Fill Downtown Storefronts

  • Attract more restaurants and entertainment so Homestead families can enjoy a night out without driving to Kendall or Cutler Bay, and keep those dollars here at home.
  • Get the CRA-owned storefronts at 204–230 N Krome Avenue open and active, with clear deadlines and public updates.
  • Create a “move-in ready” downtown program: an up-to-date vacancy list, a fast permitting path for downtown tenants, and build-out and façade help for businesses that sign leases.
  • Bring people downtown with regular events, and connect sports-campus visitors, base personnel, and national-park tourists to Krome Avenue.

Measure Progress

  • Publish downtown vacancies, new business openings, and jobs created, so residents can see the results.
05

More for Our Youth

Youth & Families

Homestead kids deserve more to do, close to home. Parents want safe, affordable activities after school, on weekends, and during the summer. Julio will work to give young people places to play, learn, and grow, and real chances to earn.

More Activities

  • Expand affordable sports leagues, after-school, and weekend programs at City parks, working with schools, nonprofits, and faith groups.
  • Make sure Homestead kids benefit from the new Sports Performance Hub, including its scholarship spots and youth programs.

Real Opportunities

  • Create summer jobs, internships, and apprenticeships for teens with local employers, the City, Miami Dade College, and CareerSource South Florida.
  • Grow mentorship programs that connect young people with local leaders, business owners, and first responders.

Work With Council

  • Invest in park improvements and youth programs through the budget, grants, and private partners.

Measure Progress

  • Track and publish program participation, new spots added, and summer jobs filled.
06

An Affordable Homestead

Cost of Living & Housing

Homestead families are feeling squeezed by rent, home prices, insurance, and taxes. As Chair of the Homestead Housing Authority, Julio knows what it takes to create homes working people can afford, and he will work to keep the cost of living in Homestead within reach.

Housing People Can Afford

  • Expand workforce and affordable housing with the Homestead Housing Authority, Miami-Dade County, state programs, and private partners, while protecting the character of established neighborhoods.
  • Support more housing choices, like accessory dwelling units where appropriate, along with the infrastructure to support them.

Keep Costs Down

  • Push to hold the line on City property taxes and spend every tax dollar carefully.
  • Keep City utility rates fair and transparent.
  • Press Tallahassee for property insurance relief for Homestead homeowners.
  • Help residents see what Amendment 3 means for their 2027 tax bill with our free property tax estimator.

Measure Progress

  • Publish the status of housing proposals, funding applications, and new homes created, so residents can see what advanced and what stalled.

Together, we can make Homestead the city we all deserve.
